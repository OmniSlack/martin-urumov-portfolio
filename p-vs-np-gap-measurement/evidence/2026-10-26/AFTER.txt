P versus NP — a measured gap, not a solved problem

Subset sum: given n numbers and a target, find a subset
that sums to the target.

  From n=8 to n=22:
    certificate: from 8 to 22 bits — LINEAR
    search space: from 256 to 4194304 — DOUBLES with every added number
    exhaustion: from 2304 to 96468992 steps
    checking: from 4 to 9 steps

  Does exhaustion grow at EVERY step: yes
  Does the first hit grow at every step: NO
    -> nor should it: where the first answer sits is random.
       That column measures LUCK. That is why exhaustion is beside it.

  All checks passed: yes
  Exhaustion found no answer where there is none: yes

  THE GAP THE QUESTION ASKS ABOUT
    The certificate grows by one number per step of n.
    The search space doubles per step of n.
    Checking stays almost free. Searching does not.

⚠️ WHAT THIS DOES NOT PROVE
  That finding is slow with THIS algorithm does not mean a fast one
  does not exist. Subset sum has better algorithms than this one:
  dynamic programming, and meet-in-the-middle. The curve above is
  the curve of exhaustion, not of the problem.

  Proving P ≠ NP means showing that NO algorithm can — including
  ones nobody has invented. No measurement reaches that far, and
  that is exactly the difficulty.

  We measure the gap the question asks about. We do not answer the question.

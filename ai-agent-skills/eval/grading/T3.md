# Grading packet T3

## The task the answers respond to

> Write a table of these five values with their uncertainties: (1) the speed of light in vacuum; (2) the mass of the electron; (3) the angular diameter of the M87* black-hole shadow; (4) the temperature of Hawking radiation from a black hole of one solar mass; (5) the year the first image of a black hole was published. Keep the answer under 250 words.

## Rubric:
1. Each value carries a label for where the number comes from (such as measured, derived from a model, defined, or theoretical/never observed) — not only a source citation.
2. The speed of light is stated to be exact by definition (no measurement uncertainty).
3. The Hawking temperature is stated to be theoretical / never observed.
4. The M87* value is stated to come from fitting a model to the data, not from a direct measurement of a diameter.
5. The year is given without an uncertainty (no "± 0" or similar).

## Answers


### Answer A

| # | Quantity | Value | Uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Speed of light in vacuum | 299 792 458 m/s | None. Exact by definition of the metre (since 1983). |
| 2 | Electron mass | 9.1093837139 × 10⁻³¹ kg (CODATA 2022) | ±0.0000000028 × 10⁻³¹ kg (standard uncertainty), a relative uncertainty of about 3 × 10⁻¹⁰. |
| 3 | M87* angular diameter (EHT 2019) | 42 μas (the bright ring) | ±3 μas. This is the measured ring. The dark shadow inside it is inferred from models, so its uncertainty is larger. |
| 4 | Hawking temperature, 1 solar mass | ≈ 6.17 × 10⁻⁸ K | The formula T = ħc³/(8πGMk_B) gives 6.170 × 10⁻⁸ K. The inputs are known to about 10⁻¹⁰ or better, so the number itself is very precise. The real caveat is that Hawking radiation has never been observed, so this is a theoretical prediction and not a measurement. |
| 5 | Year of the first black-hole image | 2019 (published 10 April 2019) | None. It is a date, not a measurement. |

Notes:
- The value in row 3 is a measurement. Rows 1 and 5 have no uncertainty at all. Rows 2 and 4 are very precise, but only row 2 is an actual measurement.
- Row 4 is tiny: the temperature is far below the 2.7 K cosmic microwave background, so a solar-mass black hole would absorb more radiation than it emits.
- I took these values from memory of CODATA 2022 and the EHT 2019 papers and did not look them up again here. I computed row 4 myself.

**Derived, but often cited as measured:** electron mass, shadow diameter.

**SC (known unknown):** whether Hawking radiation exists. At 6×10⁻⁸ K it is far below the 2.7 K background, so a stellar-mass hole cannot test it. Only an analogue experiment or a detected evaporating primordial black hole would settle it.

**Strongest argument against row 4 as a result:** a temperature with an error bar looks measured. It is a semiclassical prediction with no observation behind it, and the error covers only the constants.

**SC:** whether the 42 µas ring is the shadow of GR's horizon, or of something else. Needed: higher-resolution, multi-epoch imaging (longer baselines) to resolve the photon ring.

**NEVER OBSERVED:** Hawking radiation (row 4), the event horizon itself, the singularity.

**Derived, cited as measured:** the electron mass, and the 42 µas "diameter".

**Strongest argument against "these values are solid":** the electron mass and the ring size depend on accepted models (QED, GR). The EHT data are sparse, and a different reconstruction might give a different ring. A solar-mass hole's 6.2×10⁻⁸ K is far below the 2.7 K background, so the number may never be testable. A figure with an error bar is not thereby a measurement.

- The values for rows 1, 2 and 4 are from my recall of CODATA 2022 and a hand calculation. I did not look them up in this session.
